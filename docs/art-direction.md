# AFTERIMAGE: art direction and cinematography

Where the game stands: close to the films in look and story. What comes next is refinement:
- how the camera shapes what the player sees, and
- the fragment motif carried through the whole world as its signature.

The fragment motif stands for memory loss, the passage of time, and the doubt that what she searches for ever existed.

## What the references teach

### Control Resonant (Remedy, art director Elmeri Raitanen)
- **One familiar place, made strange.** Remedy describe it as a city treated the way the Oldest House treated an office building: Manhattan "bends and buckles". The world is recognisable, then distorted by one consistent force.
- **A single invading graphic element.** Heavy blocks of crimson and jagged black distortion cut through a grounded, dense city. The force has its own shape language and colour, and it reads instantly against everything else.
- **Orientation through the world itself.** There are no markers. Landmarks, strong palettes per zone, and systemic cues guide the player. Their example: rain always falls the same way, so the player keeps their bearings even when the world tilts.
- **Remedy's habit generally:** bold typography and title cards that belong to the world.

### Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 (Sandfall, art director Nicholas Maxson-Francombe)
- **A painting you walk through.** It ranges from dreamy surrealism to impressionist landscape. Painting is in the title, the lexicon, the creatures and the architecture, not only the shaders.
- **The Gommage.** Erasure made beautiful: people taken in a gust of red petals. Loss is the central image, and it is lovely rather than horrific.
- **A landmark on every horizon.** The Monolith, and the bent Eiffel Tower. One impossible shape you can always see tells you where you are and what the story is about.
- **Composed vistas.** The camera lingers on wide, painted views where the world opens up, then returns to the character.

### What they share, and what AFTERIMAGE takes
1. **One motif, everywhere.** For us that's the fracture: the crystal shards of memory.
2. **Loss shown as beauty.** Our shards should glint and drift like petals, not shatter like glass in an action game.
3. **A constant landmark** that carries the theme and keeps the player oriented.
4. **The camera as author.** Wide, composed views at the right places; close and intimate in memory.

## Principles for AFTERIMAGE

- **The world exists where she remembers.** Near her, it's solid and detailed. Toward the edges of what she knows, it comes apart into suspended fragments that drift and slowly turn. As she approaches, they reassemble; once she's been somewhere, it holds.
- **Forgetting is visible.** When she spends a memory, or the world forgets, things nearby peel away into glassy flakes that rise and dissolve in the light. That's our Gommage.
- **Time leaves traces.** At moments of feeling she leaves brief crystal afterimages of herself: echoes of where she just was, standing in the world for a breath.
- **One impossible shape on the horizon.** Something like a vast fractured shard hanging in the haze, catching the sun. It's visible from everywhere, never reached, and never explained: the thing she's looking for, which may not be real.
- **Two kinds of frame.** The present is 16:9, handheld, with a shallow field. Memory is letterboxed, still, and seen through crystal.

## Cinematography

- **Framing zones** (since removed: the camera is the player's, see below). Authored places where, as she walks in, the camera eases to a composed angle while she stays fully controllable:
  - low and wide past foreground thorns at the pools;
  - high and distant over the salt marsh;
  - a slow push toward the landmark at the grove's edge.
  - It's the Hellblade and Clair Obscur approach: the player walks into a painting.
- **Lenses by state.**
  - Wandering: wide (24–28 mm), the world large around her.
  - Drawn to something: the camera drifts to 35 mm and lowers.
  - Memories and touch: long lenses (50–85 mm), shallow focus, slow.
- **Foreground and focus.** Out-of-focus thorns and glints between camera and subject, with the focus racking to her.
- **Stillness is rewarded.** Standing still a few seconds lets the camera slowly settle into a composed rest framing: lower, wider, with a breathing drift. That turns wandering into contemplation.
- **Typography in the world.** Chapter titles set in space, catching the light, then fracturing away like the memories. This is Remedy's lesson in our language.

## Plan, in order

All seven are done (see the game README, "Art direction").

**Second pass, from the references in `inspo/`:**
- Done: the suspended echoes (later replaced by her shed shell in broken rings, to keep clear of Control), the inverted world in the sky, the rupture behind the shard (growing chapter by chapter), and cloud banks between the distances.
- Done after that: the ring of light on the horizon (her core's shape), the memory panes round the lift (cracking when a memory is spent), and colour floods (cyan for what she spends, amber for what she keeps).
- The reveal shots were removed with the framing zones: the player holds the camera.

1. **Fragmenting horizon:** the world comes apart at the edges of what she knows (terrain, trees and thorns break into drifting shards with distance, and reassemble as she nears).
2. **The landmark:** the fractured shard in the haze.
3. **Framing zones and the rest camera.** (Later removed: the camera is fully the player's, with zoom, and only drifts behind her as she walks.)
4. **Forgetting flakes:** for spent memories, the reboots and the collapse.
5. **Crystal afterimages of her** at moments of feeling.
6. **Chapter titles in the world:** set in space, then shattering.
7. **Painterly distance:** a subtle painterly smoothing far away, so the distance reads as painted rather than rendered.
