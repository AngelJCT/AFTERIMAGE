# AFTERIMAGE II — THE HAND THAT REMEMBERED

**Version:** 1.4 · 22 September 2026 · Corrected S26 gate geometry to match the S25 closure  
**Format:** 90-second cinematic sequel · 27 shots · three 3×3 storyboard pages  
**Working story direction:** a rescue revealed through recovered memory. This is a proposed expansion of AFTERIMAGE, not previously established backstory.

## The story

In the present, the woman explores the same biomechanical grove. A corroded gate beam falls; she catches it before she has time to think. The strain and contact trigger a memory of a child's small hand guiding her ceramic fingertip gently onto water.

The memory continues into a failing refuge. She and the child run toward an evacuation gate, but its power has failed and its heavy slab is descending. She connects the gate's emergency receiver to the port in her left shoulder. The gate rises. Feedback from the damaged mechanism begins to destroy the autobiographical memory stored in her core. She feels the damage, sees the child asking her to follow, and keeps the connection active until the child can pass.

The last handhold releases. The child's sleeve catches on a bolt. A strip of ochre cloth remains. The power surge returns through the umbilical, recognition fails, and the core shuts down. She is left curled beside the closed gate while the child continues down the safe path. The body later reboots with movement and emotional traces intact, but its accessible personal history has been lost: the condition seen in AFTERIMAGE.

Back in the present, she finds the old strip on the same bolt. She remembers that someone escaped because she stayed. She takes it and turns toward the path. Her new objective is to discover what became of the child.

## Why this expands the first film

- The line “I wondered who had taught it that” now has a visible answer: the child taught her gentle contact, and she later returned that care during the rescue.
- “Nobody came looking” remains her limited experience after waking. The sequel establishes separation; it does not establish that the child abandoned her.
- Her identity survived partly in an instinct to protect and in the way she touches things. The new story makes that instinct consequential.
- The child is shown escaping at the time of the event. The elapsed time and her present-day fate remain open. Do not promise she is still eight in the present.
- The woman does not recover an entire life in this film. She recovers one decisive event and gains a reason to search.

## Dramatic design

**WANT, present:** understand the involuntary familiarity of the gate.  
**WANT, past:** get the child through the failing exit.  
**OBSTACLE:** the gate has lost power; keeping it raised requires a connection that is damaging her memory-bearing core.  
**TURN:** she realizes the cost and chooses to maintain power until the child is clear.  
**CLOSING CHANGE:** a frightened reflex becomes a deliberate act of searching.

Opening and closing are paired around the same hand. S01 catches falling metal automatically; S27 closes gently around a physical trace and moves with purpose. The cliffhanger is specific: where did that child go?

### Fictional system rule

The protagonist's autobiographical memory is carried by the core behind the cyan shoulder interface. The emergency gate can accept power through this interface. A damaged receiver feeds a surge back into that core during the rescue. Motor routines and basic regulation can reboot; autobiographical records are harder to recover. The film expresses this with the connection, the child's reflection losing definition, the blackout, and the aftermath. This is a rule of this fictional world.

She does not need perfect advance knowledge of the erasure. S14 tells her that staying connected is harming her; S17 makes the choice to persist legible.
